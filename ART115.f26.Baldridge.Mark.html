Introduction

From the catalog:

A time-based medium, animation adopts ideas and approaches from graphic design and performance to create the illusion of motion, define characters and tell stories. Tools and techniques of 2D animation (which may include approaches to 3D) will be explored by students in this course.

We’ll take a key word from the description above and define animation as methods for creating an:
Illusion of Life

Within this context we’ll explore:

  • Rhythm

Visual or sonic, in space or time, rhythm provides structure —it’s one of the most dynamic principles of design
The exploration of rhythm pays dividends in many fields of creative activity

  • Character

From “flour sacks” and other abstract shapes, up to photorealistic human beings, character comes down to personality, a sense of autonomy, goal-seeking behavior

  • Meaning

Feeling is first. This is what we talk of when referring to the “expressiveness” of a medium
Intention, messaging, advertising? Feeling is the carrier wave on which they ride

Goals and Objectives

Instructional Goals:

  • Build a foundation in the fundamentals of animation
  • Explore the relation between character and narrative
  • Learn to tell stories with animation

Learning Objectives

  • Technical Proficiency Move shapes and colors around in space, demonstrating spacial and temporal rhythm
  • Conceptual Create and animate characters with personality, discover how character generates narrative
  • Developmental Learn to control emotional effects to create satisfying experiences

Projects

In this course we will produce three Projects

  • Abstractions gives us a chance to play with rhythm in space and time, to experiment with textures and sync sound

  • In Characters tell Stories we’ll learn that contrasting characters in carefully chosen situations generate stories on their own

  • Advance! Gives us a chance to try out some new techniques, more fully develop our ideas and projects or introduce new technologies or softwares (This may include interactivity, depending on student interest)

Evaluation

All course work will be evaluated on the basis of a 100-point semester

  • Tutorial Assignments

    • Each of these is calculated to lead to a particular Project
    • The number of these may vary, but for each of the three sections, assignments will always total 10 points (if there are five Tutorial Assignments leading up to Abstractions, each will be worth 2 points, etc)

  • Three Projects

    • As described and named above
    • Each worth a maximum of 20 points

  • The remaining 10 points are divided into:

    • Creativity Points (3)
      • Show design-centric work which engages viewers on an aesthetic level
    • Innovation Points (3)
      • Demonstrate techniques or approaches not covered in class, seek to educate yourself beyond the coursework and demonstrate that knowledge
    • Engagement Points (4)
      • Show up to and engage in classroom conversations, ask and answer questions, help your peers (this last is always the most impressive to your instructor)
