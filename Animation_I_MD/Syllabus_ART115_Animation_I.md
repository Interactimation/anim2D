## Introduction

> **From the catalog:** A time-based medium, animation adopts ideas and approaches from graphic design and performance to create the illusion of motion, define characters and tell stories. Tools and techniques of 2D animation (which may include approaches to 3D) will be explored by students in this course.

We'll take a key word from the description above and define animation as methods for creating:  
**_Illusions_ of Time and Motion**  
**_Illusions_ of Space and Mass**  
**_Illusions_ of Character and Personality**  
each corresponding to one quarter of the course —the last quarter given over to applying what we've learned in a Final Project (or Projects)

Within these contexts we'll explore:  
* **Rhythm**  

Visual or sonic, in space or time, rhythm provides structure —it's one of the most dynamic principles of design    
The exploration of rhythm pays dividends in many fields of creative activity

* **Character**  

From ["flour sacks"](https://www.animatorisland.com/hello-world/) and other abstract shapes, up to photorealistic human beings, character comes down to personality, a sense of autonomy, goal-seeking behavior 

* **Meaning**   

Feeling is first. This is what we talk of when referring to the "expressiveness" of a medium  
_Intention, messaging, advertising?_ Feeling is the carrier wave on which they ride

## Goals and Objectives

### Instructional Goals: 

1. Build a foundation in the fundamentals of animation  
2. Explore the relation between character and narrative 
3. Learn to tell stories with animation

### Learning Objectives

1. Move shapes and colors around in space, demonstrating spacial and temporal rhythm
2. Create and animate characters with personality, to tell stories
3. Generate _feelings_ in your audience

## Organization

What follows is a breakdown of work the course entails, the way it is assessed and a hypothetical schedule of the semester:

### Projects 
Students will complete four Projects worth 85% of course evaluation —these will grow out of _Research, Create_ and  _Challenge_ assignments (at east two of which will be offered in each of the first three quarters) from which learners may choose 

Project descriptions given here are preliminary and hypothetical: Detailed descriptions will be posted to the course Canvas for each, developed out of the work going on in the classroom

* **Project One:** _Flip out like a Ninja_ (17 points) 
* **Project Two:** _It Builds Character_ (17 points) 
* **Project Three:** _I Like to Move it, Move it_ (17 points) 
* **Project Four:** _Roll the Cartoon!_ (34 points —double the value of any other Project) 

> **NOTE:** Completion of all Projects _as described_ represents a score of 85 points —a solid B in my books (see: [Scale](#scale))

### Creativity, Innovation, Engagement  
The remaining 15 points of course evaluation is broken down into three categories: Creativity, Innovation, Engagement, each worth 5 points

* _Creativity_  
These points go to those who explore the expressive qualities of our work, show design and communication thinking in their Projects  
Those who think and work _beyond_ the Project earn these points 

* _Innovation_  
These go to those who don't wait for instruction but find tutorials and resources on their own  
The adoption of techniques not taught in class, the involvement of technologies different from our focus, all contribute

* _Engagement_ 
Begins with attendance and goes on to completion of at least three _Research, Create_ or _Challenge_ assignments (worth 2 points each) participation in discussion, the asking and answering of questions  
I am particularly eager to see peers helping peers —as all benefit from this kind of interaction

**Of the above** I recognize that Creativity points are the most subjective, Innovation points the most difficult to earn and Engagement the easiest —in all cases of evaluation, you should act as your own advocate
> If you feel you deserve more, say so, and _why_ —I welcome such discussion!
