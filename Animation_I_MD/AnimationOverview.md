## Meet Mr Muybridge


> Photographs stop time —Eadweard Muybridge started it again


<figure>
<img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/12/Portrait_of_Eadweard_Muybridge%2C_pioneer_of_early_action_photography.png" alt="portrait of Eadweard Muybridge" style="width:400px;">
  <figcaption>Muybridge: <i>Eadweardian</i> photographer, taken between 1872 and 1878</figcaption>
</figure>


An Englishman who emigrated to America seeking his fortune, Muybridge found it as a photographer when a former California governor —and founder of Stanford University— became obsessed with the gait of galloping horses, spending freely to discover what the human eye could hardly detect: how, when and even _if_ all four hooves were ever off the ground!


It involved invention, creative thinking, and years of work (during which time the photographer had to be defended by his patron _on the charge of murder_!) to get it right, but in June 1878 Muybridge invited reporters to watch as a horse ran down a track, breaking threads every 21 inches, each thread triggering a different camera, each shutter closing in a thousandth of a second


**Nothing like this had ever been attempted previously**


Muybridge developed the pictures in front of reporters so there'd be no charges of forgery and in one sequence of 16 pictures, the horse, Sallie Gardner, clearly shows all four hooves in the air!

Muybridge named his new process _chronophotography_ and, one year later, developed the _zoopraxiscope_ —a kind of hand-painted precursor to cinema!

## Stop-Motion

Stop motion animation refers to the trick of stopping a film camera, making a change to a scene, photographing that change and sometimes repeating the process

The "stop trick" was invented by [Georges Méliès](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PWHj1AAyXSU) it's just about the very first "special effect" in cinema!

It sometimes has a sort of comic effect —here it is being used in a poignant satire on war, in 1952s [_Neighbors_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8z95M_ktpr4)

## Get Giffy Wit It

Stephen Wilhite, who died in 2022 of complications from COVID-19 (aged 74) claimed he "never got 1 cent" for creating the Graphics Interchange Format (GIF) in June, 1987

The original format wasn't intended to work around the _proprietary_ image formats of big operators like Apple and IBM to bring "high quality" images to the, at the time, very low-bandwidth web

The format was capable of holding multiple images which could be displayed in sequence, like a slide show  

Two years later, an update allowed users to specify how long each image should be displayed, making animation possible  

It wasn't until Netscape Navigator incorporated GIFs in 1995 that the animations could be looped, and thus began _The Golden-Age of GIFing_!
